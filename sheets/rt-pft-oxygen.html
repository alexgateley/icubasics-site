PFTs and Oxygen Delivery

Spirometry, DLCO, oxygen devices, COPD targets and aerosols

Checked against current sources October 2026

Spirometry: obstruction or restriction?

LLN = lower limit of normal (z-score −1.645). Low ratio plus low TLC = mixed pattern.
FindingObstructiveRestrictive
FEV₁/FVCLowNormal or high
FVCNormal or lowLow
TLCNormal or highLow (confirms it)
RVHigh (air trapping)Low; high in neuromuscular weakness
  • Low FVC, normal ratio: restriction, air trapping or poor effort; low TLC confirms restriction.
  • Classic cutoffs: TLC ≤80% predicted = restriction, >120% = hyperinflation; RV >120% or high RV/TLC = air trapping.
  • ATS/ERS 2022 severity (z-score): mild −1.65 to −2.5, moderate −2.5 to −4, severe below −4.
  • Quality: 3 acceptable efforts; two largest FEV₁ and FVC within 0.150 L (over age 6).
Exam tip GOLD: post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC under 0.70 confirms COPD (ATS/ERS uses the LLN); FEV₁ % predicted grades it: 1 ≥80, 2 50–79, 3 30–49, 4 under 30.

Bronchodilator response and DLCO

  • ATS/ERS 2022: FEV₁ or FVC rises more than 10% of predicted.
  • Older 2005 rule, still common: at least 12% and 200 mL above baseline.
  • Withhold first: SABA 4–6 h, SAMA 12 h, LABA 24 h (once-daily 36 h), LAMA 36–48 h.
VA = alveolar volume; KCO = DLCO ÷ VA. Correct for hemoglobin.
DLCOThink of
Low; low VA and KCOInterstitial lung disease
Low; normal VAEmphysema, pulmonary vascular disease, anemia
Low; low VA, normal or high KCOChest wall or neuromuscular (poor expansion)
HighAlveolar hemorrhage, polycythemia, high blood flow, some asthma
Exam tip Emphysema = obstruction + low DLCO; asthma = obstruction + normal or high DLCO; fibrosis = restriction + low DLCO.

Oxygen devices

Low-flow: below inspiratory demand, so FiO₂ varies. High-flow: meets demand, so FiO₂ is fixed.

Typical adult exam values; sources differ, so follow your institution's protocols. Cannula: about +4% per L/min.
DeviceFlowApprox. FiO₂Type
Nasal cannula1–6 L/min24–44%Low
Simple mask5–10 L/min35–50%Low
Nonrebreather10–15 L/min60–80%Low, reservoir
VenturiMinimum on jet24–60%High
High-flow cannulaUp to 60 L/min21–100%High
  • Simple mask: at least 5 L/min to flush CO₂.
  • Reservoir masks: never let the bag collapse (nonrebreather ≥10 L/min). Partial rebreather: no valves; the first third of each exhalation refills the bag.
  • Venturi: more O₂ flow raises total flow, not FiO₂ (raise it if RR is over 30). Blocked ports raise FiO₂ and cut flow.
  • HFNC: heated and humidified; meets inspiratory demand; washes out dead space; small PEEP effect.
Exam tip Venturi math: air:O₂ = (100 − O₂%) ÷ (O₂% − 21). Total flow = O₂ flow × total parts. 40% ≈ 3:1, so 8 L/min gives about 32 L/min.

COPD and oxygen targets

  • Risk of hypercapnia (COPD, morbid obesity, neuromuscular or chest wall disease, CF): SpO₂ 88–92%.
  • Most other acutely ill adults: 94–98% (AARC, BTS).
  • Start with a 24–28% Venturi or cannula at 1–2 L/min; titrate per protocol.
  • CO₂ rises mainly from worse V/Q (lost hypoxic vasoconstriction); reduced drive and Haldane effect add.
  • Long-term O₂: PaO₂ ≤55 mm Hg or SaO₂ ≤88%; or PaO₂ 56–59 (SaO₂ 89%) with edema, cor pulmonale or Hct over 56% (Medicare; GOLD: 55–60, Hct over 55%).
Safety Never withhold O₂ from hypoxemic COPD or stop it abruptly once CO₂ has risen: rebound hypoxemia can be severe. Step down gradually with SpO₂ monitoring.

Humidity and aerosols

SituationHumidity
Low-flow O₂Usually none; AARC: consider above 4 L/min
HFNCAlways heated and humidified
Ventilator, heated33–44 mg H₂O/L, 34–41 °C at Y-piece
Ventilator, HME≥30 mg H₂O/L; not with low tidal volumes
NIVActive humidity; no HME
  • Age 4 or under or poor coordination: nebulizer or pMDI with valved holding chamber; mask, no blow-by.
  • pMDI and soft mist inhaler: slow, deep breath. DPI: fast, forceful breath; age 4 or over.
  • Large-volume nebulizer: bland aerosol for thick secretions or a bypassed airway; cool mist for upper-airway edema.
  • Mist should flow all through inspiration; if it vanishes, total flow is too low (likelier at high FiO₂).
Safety Hypercapnic COPD: use an air-driven nebulizer with O₂ by cannula during treatment. An O₂-driven nebulizer mask delivers about 60%.

Oxygen hazards

  • Toxicity: dose- and time-dependent; avoid FiO₂ over 0.6 unless needed for survival.
  • Absorption atelectasis behind blocked airways, even at FiO₂ 0.3–0.5.
  • Preterm infants: retinopathy of prematurity, chronic lung disease.
  • Infection risk from humidifier and nebulizer reservoirs.
Safety Fire: O₂ supports combustion. No smoking, flames or heat sources nearby; secure cylinders against falls.
