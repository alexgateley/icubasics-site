DKA and HHS

2024 consensus criteria, treatment order, potassium, euglycemic DKA and monitoring

Checked against current sources October 2026

Download one-page PDFLetter landscape, ready to print or keep with your notes

Diagnosis (2024 consensus)

DKA needs all three: D (diabetes or high glucose), K (ketonemia), A (acidosis). HHS needs all four of its criteria; altered mental status is no longer one. Over a third of crises are mixed.

BOHB = beta-hydroxybutyrate. Effective osmolality (mOsm/kg) = 2 × Na⁺ + glucose ÷ 18.

Exam tip 2024 changes: DKA glucose 250 → 200 or known diabetes; blood BOHB preferred; HHS effective osmolality 320 → 300; hold insulin if K⁺ <3.5 (was 3.3); dextrose at 250 (was 200). Older books use 2009 numbers.

  • DKA resolved: BOHB <0.6 mmol/L plus venous pH ≥7.3 or HCO₃⁻ ≥18
  • HHS resolved: osmolality <300, glucose <250, urine >0.5 mL/kg/h, mentation improving
  • Do not use the anion gap or urine ketones to call resolution

Treatment order

Typical adult doses for exam study. Follow your institution's protocols.

  1. Fluids: 0.9% saline or balanced crystalloid 500–1,000 mL/h for 2–4 h (smaller boluses, e.g. 250 mL, if older, heart failure or dialysis)
  2. Potassium: check before insulin (table below)
  3. Insulin: regular IV 0.1 unit/kg/h; 0.1 unit/kg bolus (IM or IV) only if IV access is delayed. Uncomplicated mild to moderate DKA may use SC rapid-acting insulin every 1–2 h
  4. Glucose <250 mg/dL: add D5–D10, lower insulin to 0.05 unit/kg/h, aim for about 200
  5. Bridge: SC basal insulin 1–2 h before the drip stops
  6. Treat the trigger: infection, missed insulin, new diabetes, MI, stroke, pancreatitis
Goal K⁺ 4–5 mEq/L.
K⁺ (mEq/L)Action
<3.5Hold insulin; give K⁺ about 10 mEq/h until >3.5
3.5–5.0Start insulin; add 20–30 mEq K⁺ per liter
>5.0Start insulin; no K⁺ yet, recheck
Safety Never start insulin with K⁺ below 3.5: it drives K⁺ into cells and can cause fatal arrhythmias and respiratory weakness.
Exam tip Priority: fluids, potassium, then insulin. Glucose falls below 250 long before ketoacidosis clears: add dextrose and keep insulin running.

Bicarbonate, phosphate and HHS pacing

  • Bicarbonate: not routine; consider only if pH <7.0 (2009: <6.9)
  • Phosphate: replace only if severely low with weakness or respiratory or cardiac compromise
  • HHS insulin: 0.05 unit/kg/h; 0.1 if mixed DKA–HHS (BOHB ≥3.0, pH <7.30 or HCO₃⁻ <18)
  • HHS limits: glucose fall ≤90–120 mg/dL per h, osmolality fall 3–8 mOsm/kg per h, Na⁺ fall ≤10 mEq/L in 24 h
  • Na⁺ rises about 1.6 mEq/L per 100 mg/dL glucose fall; that alone is no reason for 0.45% saline

Euglycemic DKA and SGLT2 inhibitors

  • Glucose <200 mg/dL with DKA ketones and acidosis; about 10% of DKA
  • Most cases now: SGLT2 inhibitors (-gliflozins). Also pregnancy, fasting, alcohol, liver failure
  • Check ketones and pH in any ill SGLT2 inhibitor user, whatever the glucose
  • Treat as DKA, but start D5–D10 with the first fluids
  • Stop SGLT2 inhibitors on admission; hold at least 3 days before elective surgery (ertugliflozin 4)
  • After DKA: no routine restart in T2D; not recommended in T1D
Exam tip Classic trap: vomiting, Kussmaul breathing and a high anion gap with glucose 160 on empagliflozin. Glucose under 200 does not rule out DKA.

Complications and nursing monitoring

Safety Cerebral edema: rare in adults (<0.1%), about 30% fatal; more common in children. Report new headache or vomiting after treatment starts, falling LOC, slowing heart rate or incontinence at once. Mannitol or hypertonic saline treats it.
  • Glucose every 1–2 h; hypoglycemia is common during treatment
  • K⁺ 2 h after insulin starts, then every 4 h; about half drop below 3.5; cardiac monitoring
  • Electrolytes, phosphate, BOHB, venous pH (and osmolality in HHS) every 4 h
  • Strict I&O; watch for fluid overload in older, cardiac or renal patients
  • Serial neuro checks; VTE prophylaxis unless contraindicated
  • Discharge teaching: sick-day rules, never skip basal insulin, ketone checks
