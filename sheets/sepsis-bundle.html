Sepsis and Septic Shock Bundle

Sepsis-3 definitions, the Hour-1 bundle and SSC 2026 changes for exam review

Checked against current sources October 2026

Definitions and screening

TermExam definition
SepsisSuspected or confirmed infection plus acute SOFA rise ≥2
Septic shockPressor for MAP ≥65 plus lactate >2 mmol/L (18 mg/dL) after adequate fluids
qSOFA (2 of 3)RR ≥22, altered mentation, SBP ≤100
SIRS (2 or more)T >38 or <36 °C; HR >90; RR >20 or PaCO2 <32; WBC >12K, <4K or >10% bands
Exam tip qSOFA predicts a poor outcome but does not diagnose sepsis. SSC 2026 strongly prefers NEWS, NEWS2, MEWS or SIRS over qSOFA as a single screening tool.

Hour-1 bundle

Start at recognition. Not every element must finish within 60 minutes. This is the 2018 SSC bundle, still the usual exam framework; SSC 2026 gives the 30 mL/kg over the first 3 h.

  1. Measure lactate; repeat if >2 mmol/L
  2. Blood cultures before antibiotics
  3. Broad-spectrum antibiotics
  4. 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥4
  5. Vasopressors if hypotensive during or after fluids, for MAP ≥65
SituationAntibiotics (SSC 2026)
Possible shock, or probable/definite sepsisImmediately, ideally within 1 h
Possible sepsis, no shockRapid workup; within 3 h if infection is still a concern
Exam tip "What first?" Cultures, then antibiotics, unless drawing cultures would delay antibiotics by 45 min or more (SSC 2021). SSC 2026 (new, strong): blood cultures as soon as possible, ideally before antimicrobials.

Fluids and reassessment

  • At least 30 mL/kg crystalloid in the first 3 h (actual weight; adjusted or ideal if BMI >30)
  • Balanced crystalloid (LR, Plasma-Lyte) over 0.9% saline; saline in TBI
  • Albumin: SSC 2026 suggests crystalloids alone; may fit after large volumes or in cirrhosis; avoid in TBI. No starches or gelatin
  • Guide more fluid with dynamic measures (passive leg raise, SVV, PPV)
  • Trend lactate and capillary refill time (goal 3 s or less)
Safety Reassess after every bolus. New crackles, rising oxygen needs, JVD or edema suggest fluid intolerance: stop the bolus and notify the provider.

Vasopressors and MAP

StepAgentExam point
1stNorepinephrineFirst-line; may start peripherally
2ndVasopressinFixed 0.03 units/min, added as NE rises; not titrated
3rdEpinephrineIf MAP still low on NE plus vasopressin
Cardiac dysfunctionAdd dobutamine, or epi aloneLow output despite fluids and pressors
  • MAP goal 65 mm Hg; age 65 or older: 60 to 65 (new in 2026)
  • NE over dopamine (strong): dopamine causes more arrhythmias
  • Vasopressin: SSC 2026 sets no NE threshold (panel median start 0.3 mcg/kg/min); SSC 2021 said 0.25 to 0.5
  • Cardiac dysfunction: NE or epi may be the first pressor (new in 2026)
  • NE can start during fluids if hypoperfusion is life-threatening
Safety Peripheral NE: SSC 2026 suggests starting it peripherally but says data are insufficient to set a duration, dose, catheter size or site. SSC 2021 advised a short period through a vein at or above the antecubital fossa, as many protocols still do. Check the site often for extravasation and follow your institution's protocol.

Steroids, source control and more

  • IV hydrocortisone, with or without fludrocortisone, if the NE need persists; not a last resort
  • SSC 2026 sets no dose or start threshold; most panelists still give 200 mg/day. Classic exam answer (SSC 2021): 50 mg every 6 h once NE or epi is ≥0.25 mcg/kg/min for at least 4 h
  • Source control ideally within 6 h
  • Insulin at glucose ≥180 mg/dL; typical target 144 to 180 (SSC 2021)
  • Bicarbonate: not for lactic acidemia alone; suggested if pH ≤7.2 with AKI
  • Not advised: IV vitamin C, beta-blockers, antipyretics to improve outcomes

Doses are typical adult values for exam study. Follow your institution's protocols.

CMS SEP-1 vs SSC

ItemSepsis-3 / SSCCMS SEP-1
TermsSepsis, septic shock (severe sepsis retired)Severe sepsis, septic shock
Sepsis criteriaInfection + SOFA rise ≥2Infection + 2 SIRS + organ dysfunction (e.g., lactate >2)
ShockPressors plus lactate >2Hypotension after fluids, or lactate ≥4
TimingHour-1 bundle3-hour and 6-hour bundles
Exam tip "Severe sepsis" or a 3- or 6-hour bundle in the stem signals SEP-1. Its 6-hour items: pressors for persistent hypotension, a documented perfusion reassessment, and a repeat lactate if the first was over 2.

Pediatric note (Phoenix 2024)

  • Sepsis: suspected infection plus Phoenix score ≥2 (respiratory, cardiovascular, coagulation, neuro); under 18, not preterm newborns
  • Septic shock: sepsis plus ≥1 cardiovascular point (severe hypotension for age, lactate 5 or more, or any vasoactive)
  • SSC 2026 (children) describes Phoenix but requires no set criteria; it uses probable sepsis and suspected septic shock
  • SSC 2026: antimicrobials ideally within 1 h for suspected septic shock; for probable sepsis without shock, rapid workup, then within 3 h if sepsis is substantiated
  • 10 to 20 mL/kg boluses with reassessment; up to 40 to 60 mL/kg in hour 1 where ICU care exists
