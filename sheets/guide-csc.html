Who can take it

  • Current, unencumbered U.S. RN or APRN license
  • Current ABSNC- or NCCA-accredited specialty certification in direct adult care (e.g., CCRN Direct Care or Tele-critical Care, PCCN Direct Care, CEN, CNOR, CPAN); CSC attaches to it, as in CCRN-CSC
  • Practice hours in a U.S., Canadian or comparable facility, by one of two options:
Direct care of acutely or critically ill adults. *Part of the total: adult cardiac surgery patients in the first 48 hours post-op. Orientation hours count only when the patient assignment is yours.

How to study

Every day, clear Daily Review first: misses return tomorrow, and known questions space out toward exam day.

  1. Week 1: set your exam date, read the test plan, and take a baseline CSC Mock Exam.
  2. Weeks 1-2: Therapeutic Interventions, with subject practice, Level Up, and the Vasopressors and Inotropes, IABP Timing, ECMO, Impella and CRRT One-Pagers.
  3. Weeks 3-4: Complications, with the Shock Types, Electrolyte Imbalances and Rhythm Gallery One-Pagers.
  4. Week 5: Procedures and Monitoring, with the Hemodynamic Normals and ABG Interpretation One-Pagers.
  5. Weeks 6-7: Weakest Subject and Trouble Questions, plus two timed Mock Exams.
  6. Week 8: let final-week review bring back past misses, skim your One-Pagers, and rest the day before.

Test-day tips

  • Bring a current government photo ID with signature that matches your AACN record; no military or temporary ID.
  • Test center: if you arrive more than 15 minutes late, you will not be admitted. Scratch paper is available on request.
  • Remote: check in 15-30 minutes early and expect a room scan. No scratch paper; you get a digital notepad.
  • Pace about 80 seconds per question, bookmark hard items, and answer every question. Guessing is not penalized.
  • Two 5-minute breaks are allowed, but the clock keeps running.
  • Drugs appear by generic name. While AACN makes the switch, some items also show the trade name.
