ABG Interpretation in 6 Steps

pH, PaCO₂, HCO₃⁻, compensation, anion gap and oxygenation, with worked examples

Normal values

Typical adult arterial values at sea level.

PaO₂80–100 mm HgRoom air; some labs 75–100

Anion gapAbout 12Lab ranges vary
Safety Sampling: Allen test before a radial stick; no air bubbles; analyze promptly; hold firm pressure, longer if anticoagulated; after O₂ or vent changes, wait per protocol.

The 6 steps

  1. pH: below 7.35 acidemia, above 7.45 alkalemia.
  2. PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻: mark each high, normal or low.
  3. Primary disorder: the value that explains the pH (ROME).
  4. Compensation: compare the other value with the expected range.
  5. Metabolic acidosis? Anion gap, albumin correction, delta ratio.
  6. Oxygenation: PaO₂, P/F ratio, A–a gradient.
Exam tip ROME: Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal (pH versus PaCO₂ or HCO₃⁻). NCLEX stages: uncompensated (only the primary value off), partially compensated (both off, pH abnormal), fully compensated (pH normal; under 7.40 leans acid).
Safety Compensation seldom returns pH fully to normal. A normal pH with abnormal PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻ is often two disorders at once.

Expected compensation

Row 1 is Winter's formula. A value well outside the range suggests a second disorder.
PrimaryExpected responseComplete in
Metabolic acidosisPaCO₂ = 1.5 × HCO₃⁻ + 8 (±2)12–24 h
Metabolic alkalosisPaCO₂ up 0.6–0.7 per 1 HCO₃⁻ rise; rarely over 5524–36 h
Acute resp. acidosisHCO₃⁻ up 1 per 10 PaCO₂ riseImmediate
Chronic resp. acidosisHCO₃⁻ up about 4 per 103–5 days
Acute resp. alkalosisHCO₃⁻ down 2 per 10 PaCO₂ fallImmediate
Chronic resp. alkalosisHCO₃⁻ down 4–5 per 102–3 days
Safety DKA: a PaCO₂ above the Winter's range signals a tiring patient. Acute asthma: PaCO₂ runs low early; normal or above 45 signals impending respiratory failure. Escalate early.

Anion gap and delta ratio

  • Anion gap = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)
  • Corrected gap = gap + 2.5 × (4 − albumin in g/dL)
  • Delta ratio = (corrected gap − 12) ÷ (24 − HCO₃⁻), a rough guide
  • Ratio under 1: added normal-gap acidosis
  • Ratio 1–2: pure high-gap acidosis
  • Ratio over 2: added metabolic alkalosis
Exam tip Exams test which acidoses raise the gap: DKA, lactate and toxic alcohols do; diarrhea and large-volume saline do not. Kussmaul breathing is the classic compensation cue.

Oxygenation

  • P/F = PaO₂ ÷ FiO₂ (as a decimal)
  • ARDS (intubated, PEEP 5 or more): mild over 200 to 300, moderate over 100 to 200, severe 100 or less
  • SpO₂/FiO₂ 315 or less (if SpO₂ 97% or less) can stand in for P/F 300 (2024 definition)
  • PAO₂ = FiO₂ × (760 − 47) − PaCO₂ ÷ 0.8 at sea level; room air about 150 − 1.25 × PaCO₂
  • A–a gradient = PAO₂ − PaO₂; normal about age ÷ 4 + 4
  • Low PaO₂ + normal A–a: hypoventilation or low inspired O₂ (altitude)
  • Low PaO₂ + high A–a: lung problem (V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion)

Common causes

DisorderCommon causes
Respiratory acidosisOpioids, sedatives, COPD, asthma fatigue, neuromuscular weakness
Respiratory alkalosisPain, anxiety, hypoxemia, PE, early sepsis, pregnancy, salicylates
High-gap metabolic acidosisLactate, ketoacidosis, kidney failure, toxic alcohols, salicylates
Normal-gap metabolic acidosisDiarrhea, large-volume saline, renal tubular acidosis
Metabolic alkalosisVomiting, NG suction, diuretics, hypokalemia
Safety Potassium: acidemia tends to shift K⁺ out of cells, alkalemia shifts it in. In DKA, serum K⁺ may be normal or high despite a total-body deficit, and it falls once insulin starts.

Worked examples

1. DKA, Kussmaul breathing: pH 7.22, PaCO₂ 25, HCO₃⁻ 10, Na⁺ 138, Cl⁻ 100, albumin 4.0

  • Metabolic acidosis; Winter's 23 (21–25), PaCO₂ 25 fits
  • Gap 138 − 110 = 28; delta ratio 16 ÷ 14 ≈ 1.1
  • Answer: high-gap metabolic acidosis, compensated as expected (NCLEX: partially compensated)

2. Opioid overdose, age 24, room air: pH 7.26, PaCO₂ 60, HCO₃⁻ 26, PaO₂ 68

  • Respiratory acidosis; acute rule predicts 26, matches
  • A–a: 150 − 75 − 68 = 7, normal for age
  • Answer: acute (uncompensated) respiratory acidosis; hypoxemia from hypoventilation

3. Septic shock, 5 L saline, ventilated, PEEP 10, FiO₂ 0.6: pH 7.29, PaCO₂ 30, HCO₃⁻ 14, Na⁺ 140, Cl⁻ 112, albumin 2.0, PaO₂ 90

  • Metabolic acidosis; Winter's 29 (27–31), PaCO₂ 30 fits
  • Corrected gap 14 + 5 = 19; delta ratio 7 ÷ 10 = 0.7
  • Answer: high-gap (likely lactate) plus normal-gap (saline) acidosis; P/F 150, moderate ARDS range
