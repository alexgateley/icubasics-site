Rhythm Gallery

21 drawn strips: how to name each rhythm and the first move exams expect

Checked against current sources October 2026

Sinus, atrial and junctional

Read in order: rate, regularity, P waves, PR, QRS. Normal PR 0.12–0.20 s; narrow QRS under 0.12 s. Doses are typical adult exam values; follow your institution's protocols.

Safety AF 48 h or more: 3 weeks of anticoagulation, or imaging to rule out clot, before elective cardioversion; 4 weeks after. Never delay an unstable patient.
Safety AF with WPW (fast, irregular, wide): no AV-nodal blockers (adenosine, diltiazem, beta-blockers, digoxin, amiodarone). Unstable: cardiovert. Stable: procainamide or ibutilide.

AV blocks

Exam tip Mobitz I vs II: the PR lengthens then resets in type I; it stays constant in type II, which exams pace. A 2:1 block cannot be typed from one strip.
Safety In type II and complete block, atropine often fails; do not let it delay pacing. Dopamine 5–20 mcg/kg/min or epinephrine 2–10 mcg/min can bridge.

Ventricular

Exam tip Exams test the VT pulse check: no pulse, defibrillate; a pulse but unstable, synchronized cardioversion; stable, antiarrhythmic and expert help.
Safety Polymorphic VT and VF need unsynchronized shocks; sync mode may not fire. For synchronized cardioversion, sedate if possible and confirm sync before each shock.

Pacing

Safety R-on-T pacing can trigger VF. Transcutaneous pacing hurts: give analgesia and sedation, and check a femoral pulse, as muscle twitch mimics a carotid pulse.

ST and T changes

Exam tip Calcium protects the heart but does not lower K. Up to half of patients with a high K have a normal ECG.
