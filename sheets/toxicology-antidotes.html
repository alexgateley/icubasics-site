Toxidromes and Antidotes

Toxidromes, antidotes and decontamination for exam review

Checked against current sources October 2026

Toxidromes at a glance

ToxidromeVitalsPupils, skinClues
Sympathomimetic (cocaine, meth)High HR, BP, tempDilated; sweatyAgitation, seizures
Anticholinergic (diphenhydramine, jimsonweed)High HR, tempDilated; hot, dry, redDelirium, urinary retention, quiet bowel
Cholinergic (organophosphates, nerve agents)Low HR (can be high)Pinpoint; wetSLUDGE, wheeze, fasciculations
OpioidLow RRPinpoint; normalComa, shallow breaths
Sedative-hypnotic (benzos, barbiturates)Normal to lowVariable; normalSlurred speech, ataxia

Cholinergic DUMBELS: diarrhea, urination, miosis, bradycardia/bronchorrhea/bronchospasm, emesis, lacrimation, salivation. The three B's kill.

Exam tip Sympathomimetic and anticholinergic are both hot, fast and dilated. Sweaty skin means sympathomimetic; dry skin means anticholinergic.
Safety Sympathomimetic crisis: sedate (often benzodiazepines), avoid prolonged restraint without sedation, cool fast; ice-water immersion is preferred (AHA 2025).

Serotonin syndrome vs NMS

FeatureSerotonin syndromeNMS
TriggerSSRI, SNRI, MAOI, tramadolAntipsychotics, metoclopramide; stopping levodopa
OnsetHours, usually within 24 hOver days
MusclesClonus, hyperreflexia, tremorSevere rigidity, reflexes decreased
OtherMydriasis, diarrhea, shiveringHigh fever, high CK
TreatmentStop drug, benzodiazepines, cooling, cyproheptadineStop drug, cooling, benzodiazepines, bromocriptine, dantrolene

Cool physically; antipyretics do not work in serotonin syndrome.

Exam tip Both cause fever, sweating and confusion. Speed of onset and clonus versus rigidity tell them apart.

Reversal antidotes

Doses on this page are typical adult doses for exam study; follow your institution's or agency's protocols.

PoisonAntidote, typical adult doseExam point
OpioidsNaloxone 0.2–2 mg IV/IM/IO or 2–4 mg nasal, every 2–3 minGoal is breathing; may wear off first
BenzodiazepinesFlumazenil 0.2 mg IV, titrated to 1 mgRarely used; no role in arrest
OrganophosphatesAtropine 1–2 mg IV, doubled every 5 min; pralidoxime 1–2 g IVAtropine endpoint: clear chest, not pupils
Methanol, ethylene glycolFomepizole 15 mg/kg IV, then 10 mg/kg every 12 hBlocks alcohol dehydrogenase
Cyanide (smoke inhalation)Hydroxocobalamin 5 g IV over 15 min, plus 100% oxygenPreferred over nitrite if CO likely; red skin, urine
Anticholinergic deliriumPhysostigmine 1 mg slow IV over 5 minAvoid with TCA or QRS over 100 ms
  • EMS DuoDote (atropine plus pralidoxime): 1 autoinjector IM if mild, 3 rapidly if severe.
  • Discharge opioid overdose patients with naloxone and teaching (AHA 2025).
Safety Flumazenil can trigger seizures in benzodiazepine-dependent patients or with TCA co-ingestion. Physostigmine has caused asystole in severe TCA poisoning.
Safety Opioid arrest: CPR with breaths comes first; naloxone may be given if it does not delay CPR (AHA 2025). Xylazine sedation may persist after naloxone; support breathing.

Acetaminophen and NAC

  • Early symptoms are vague; liver failure appears days later.
  • Level at 4 h or later; on or above 150 mcg/mL at 4 h: give NAC.
  • Over 200 mg/kg or 10 g and no level by 8 h: start NAC.
  • IV NAC, 3-bag: 150 mg/kg over 1 h, 50 mg/kg over 4 h, 100 mg/kg over 16 h.
  • Rash or wheeze on IV NAC: pause, antihistamine, restart; flushing alone often settles.
  • Extended-release or unknown time: repeat levels, call poison control.
Exam tip Exams test the 4-hour level, the 150 line and the 8-hour window.

Safety Potassium drops with bicarbonate, high-dose insulin and digoxin Fab. During bicarbonate, avoid pH above 7.50–7.55 and sodium above 150–155 mEq/L.

Beyond antidotes: ECMO, MCS, dialysis

  • VA-ECMO: reasonable for poison-induced cardiogenic shock, dysrhythmias or arrest despite maximal therapy; unproven in vasodilatory shock. Call early.
  • Sympathomimetic cardiogenic shock: IABP or VA-ECMO is reasonable.
  • Newer percutaneous pumps (such as Impella) may be considered; no graded AHA advice.
  • Dialysis clears toxic alcohols, lithium and salicylates, not TCAs.
  • Intermittent hemodialysis is preferred; CRRT if IHD is unavailable.

Decontamination

  • Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222, free, 24/7.
  • Activated charcoal: consider within 1 h, not routinely; adult dose 50–100 g.
  • Never with an unprotected airway (aspiration risk).
  • Charcoal misses alcohols, caustics, iron, lithium; not for hydrocarbons.
  • Whole bowel irrigation: sustained-release pills, metals, drug packets.
  • Organophosphate on skin: PPE, remove clothing, wash.
