ECMO Essentials

VA vs VV, the circuit, monitoring, troubleshooting and ECPR for CCRN, CMC, CSC and transport exams

Checked against current sources October 2026

VA vs VV at a glance

FeatureVVVA
SupportsLungs onlyHeart and lungs
Circuit vs heartIn series; native heart must pumpIn parallel; bypasses heart and lungs
Typical useARDS P/F <80 despite proning; pH <7.25 + PaCO₂ ≥60Refractory cardiogenic shock, ECPR, massive PE, poisoning
CannulationFem + IJ, or dual-lumen RIJFem-fem; axillary; central post-op
Usual SaO₂Often 80–90%Check right radial (femoral VA)

Exam tip Exam favorite: VV supports only the lungs; a failing heart needs inotropes or conversion to VA.

The circuit and its controls

Drainage → pump → oxygenator (with heater) → return. Pre-pump tubing is under negative pressure: an open port pulls in air.

ControlMain effect
Blood flow (pump RPM)Main oxygenation lever; on VA, also MAP
Sweep gas flowCO₂ removal: more sweep, lower PaCO₂
FdO₂ (gas blender)Post-oxygenator PO₂; lower on VA to avoid hyperoxia
Exam tip Exams pair blood flow with O₂ and sweep with CO₂. Lower a high PaCO₂ slowly: a drop over 50% near cannulation is linked to brain injury.

Monitoring and anticoagulation

  • Drainage pressure more negative at same RPM = poor drainage; pump hemolysis beyond −300 mm Hg
  • Post-oxygenator PO₂ over 300 mm Hg on FdO₂ 1.0 = working membrane
  • VA: pulse pressure shows native ejection; drainage sat reflects SvO₂ (aim over 66%)
  • Limb NIRS over 50% (ideally 60%), under 20% side-to-side gap
  • VA wean signs: pulse pressure >10, MAP >65 at 2–2.5 L/min, minimal pressors
LabExam value
Anti-Xa on UFH0.3–0.7 IU/mL
aPTTAbout 60–90 s (center-specific)
Plasma free HbUnder 10 mg/dL; over 50, find the cause
Other hemolysis clues↑LDH, ↑indirect bilirubin, ↓haptoglobin, pink urine

UFH is usual; bivalirudin or argatroban if HIT. High free Hb, bilirubin or lipids falsely lower anti-Xa. Follow your institution's protocols.

Limb ischemia (VA)Cool, pale leg; low NIRSDistal perfusion cannula (6–8 Fr) at cannulation

Air embolismBubbles, detector alarmStop pump, clamp near patient
Safety Air or decannulation: stop the pump, clamp the lines near the patient, set emergency vent settings, call the ECMO team; press on bleeding sites. On VA, support stops and a stopped pump can flow backward: prepare for CPR and pressors.

Exam tip Recirculation: bright red drainage blood, high pre-oxygenator saturation, and SaO₂ that falls as flow rises.

Nursing care and transport

  • Routine circuit checks: flows, pressures, sweep, FdO₂, flashlight
  • Cannulas secured in 2+ places; check sites; minimize punctures
  • Neuro checks every 1–4 h with pupils; distal pulses (Doppler), color, NIRS
  • Heater masks fever: watch WBC, hemodynamics and sites
  • VV lung rest: plateau ≤25 cm H₂O, PEEP ≥10, low rate
  • Transport: gas for the whole trip, backup power, hand crank, clamps
  • Pump and oxygenator at patient level; trace lines before, during and after moves; plan heat

ECMO in CPR (ECPR)

  • AHA (2a, 2023, still current): reasonable for refractory arrest in select patients within a trained, equipped system
  • AHA 2025: reasonable to set selection criteria, use experienced percutaneous cannulators, regionalize (2a); intra-arrest transport may be considered for highly selected OHCA (2b)
  • Better outcomes: bystander CPR, shockable rhythm, intermittent ROSC or signs of life, short low-flow time
  • Also 2a in 2025: pregnancy, PE, hypothermia, refractory poisoning, anaphylaxis, cath lab arrest
  • ELSO: consider after 10–15 min of failed CPR, cannulate after 10–20 min, flow within 60 min; CPR continues until flow ≥3 L/min
  • After ECPR: right-arm arterial line, SpO₂ 92–97%, MAP ≥60 but <80; watch for LV distension
