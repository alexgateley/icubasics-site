Newborn Care and Resuscitation

Apgar, NRP 9th edition steps, SpO2 targets and newborn vital signs for exam review

Checked against current sources October 2026

Download one-page PDFLetter landscape, ready to print or keep with your notes

Birth and initial steps

  1. At birth: start the cord plan
  2. Term, good tone, breathing or crying? All yes: skin-to-skin, routine care
  3. Any no: warm, dry, position the airway, stimulate; clear the airway only if obstructed
  4. Apnea, gasping or HR below 100: ventilate within the first minute
  5. HR 100 or more but labored breathing or persistent cyanosis: pulse oximetry, O2 if needed, consider CPAP (best evidence in preterm)
Cord option2025 guidance
Defer clamping 60 s or moreMost babies not needing immediate resuscitation
Intact cord milkingMay be reasonable if nonvigorous at 35 to 42 wk
No cord milkingUnder 28 wk (IVH risk)
Exam tip Suction is not routine, even with meconium: a nonvigorous baby gets ventilation, not tracheal suctioning.
Safety Keep 36.5 to 37.5 °C; both cold and overheating harm. Preterm: plastic wrap, radiant warmer, room 23 °C or warmer.

Ventilation and MR SOPA

A rising HR shows ventilation works. Each 30-s delay in starting it raised death or prolonged stay by about 16%.

Rate30 to 60 breaths/min

Exam tip Older items may say PPV and key NRP 8th: 40 to 60/min, a 1-min SpO2 target, PIP 20 to 25, clamping at 30 to 60 s. Courses switched by June 1, 2026.

Compressions, epinephrine and volume

  • HR below 60 after 30 s of ventilation that moves the chest: compressions
  • Ventilate via ETT; laryngeal mask if intubation fails (34 wk or more)
  • 3:1 ratio: 90 compressions + 30 breaths/min
  • 2-thumb encircling hands, lower third of sternum, one-third chest depth
  • 100% O2; wean to SpO2 targets once HR recovers
  • HR below 60 after 60 s of compressions: epinephrine every 3 to 5 min
  • UVC preferred; IO if UVC not possible (common in EMS)
Drug, routeTypical doseExam point
Epinephrine IV/IO0.01 to 0.03 mg/kg (NRP start: 0.02)Then 3 mL NS flush
Epinephrine via ETT0.05 to 0.1 mg/kg (NRP: 0.1)Only until access; then IV/IO at once
NS or O-negative blood10 to 20 mL/kg over 5 to 10 min; repeat if neededSuspected blood loss; HR below 60 despite epi
Safety Exam-study doses; follow your institution's or agency's protocols. Newborn epinephrine is 0.1 mg/mL (IV/IO 0.1 to 0.3 mL/kg; ETT 0.5 to 1 mL/kg), never 1 mg/mL.

Still below 60: think hypovolemia or pneumothorax. No HR by about 20 min despite all steps: team and family may discuss redirecting care. Check glucose after advanced resuscitation.

Apgar score

  • Score at 1 and 5 min
  • At 5 min: 7 to 10 reassuring; 4 to 6 moderately abnormal; 0 to 3 low
  • Below 7 at 5 min: rescore every 5 min up to 20 min
Exam tip Never wait for the Apgar to start resuscitation. Acrocyanosis scores 1 and is normal early.
