ACS and STEMI

Territories, time goals, first drugs and complications

Checked against current sources October 2026

ACS spectrum and STEMI criteria

TypeTroponinECG
Unstable anginaNot elevatedNormal or ST-T changes
NSTEMIRise/fall above 99th percentileNo ST elevation
STEMIRises; never wait for itNew ST elevation
  • ST elevation ≥1 mm in 2 contiguous leads; V2-V3: ≥2 mm men 40+, ≥2.5 mm men under 40, ≥1.5 mm women
  • ST depression in V1-V3: get V7-V9; ≥0.5 mm elevation = posterior STEMI
  • Inferior STEMI: get V4R; ≥0.5 mm elevation = RV involvement
PatternMeaning
LBBB or paced with concordant ST changes (Sgarbossa)STEMI equivalent; new LBBB alone is not
de Winter T wavesProximal LAD occlusion: cath lab now
Wellens T waves V2-V3, pain-freeCritical LAD; cath, no stress test
aVR elevation, diffuse ST depressionLeft main or 3-vessel; no lytic

12-lead territories

WallLeadsArteryWatch for
Septal/anteriorV1-V4LADPump failure, VSD, LV thrombus
LateralI, aVL, V5-V6LCx or diagonalOften with other walls
InferiorII, III, aVFRCA (80%) or LCxBradycardia, AV block, RV MI
Right ventricleV4R; clues V1 elevation, III > IIProximal RCAHypotension: IV fluid, no nitrates
PosteriorV7-V9LCx or RCAMissed without posterior leads

Inferior MI block: nodal, transient, narrow escape; atropine may help. Anterior MI block: infranodal, wide unstable escape; prepare to pace. New BBB alone: pads on, watch.

Exam tip Remember 10, 90, 120, 30. EMS: transmit the 12-lead, notify early, bypass to a PCI center.

Initial medications

Typical adult doses for exam study; follow your institution's or agency's protocols. EMTs: aspirin and assisting with the patient's own nitroglycerin.

DrugTypical doseKey point
Aspirin162-325 mg chewed; then 75-100 mg dailyEven if on daily aspirin
Ticagrelor180 mg loadPreferred over clopidogrel; dyspnea
Prasugrel60 mg loadAlso preferred; PCI only; no stroke/TIA; usually avoid 75+
Clopidogrel600 mg for PCI; 300 mg with lyticLytic at 75+: 75 mg, no load
Heparin (UFH)PCI: 70-100 units/kg to ACT; lytic: 60 units/kg (max 4,000)Or bivalirudin, enoxaparin
Nitroglycerin0.4 mg SL every 5 min, max 3Only if SBP ≥90
OxygenOnly if SpO2 under 90%No routine use
Beta-blockerOral, within 24 hNot with HF, shock risk or block
Safety No nitrates: SBP under 90 (or 30 below baseline), RV infarct, sildenafil or vardenafil in past 24 h, tadalafil 48 h, avanafil 12 h. Opioids only if nitrates fail. No NSAIDs except aspirin.

Fibrinolysis and reperfusion

Absolute contraindications:

  • Any prior intracranial hemorrhage
  • Cerebral AVM or malignant brain tumor
  • Ischemic stroke within 3 months
  • Suspected aortic dissection
  • Active bleeding or bleeding disorder (not menses)
  • Head/face trauma within 3 months; brain/spine surgery within 2 months
  • BP over 180/110 despite emergency treatment

Relative: BP over 180/110 on arrival, oral anticoagulant, recent internal bleeding, pregnancy, prolonged CPR. No lytic for ST depression alone unless posterior STEMI. Reperfusion signs at 60-90 min:

  • Pain relief
  • ST elevation down ≥50% (2025 ACC/AHA: ≥70% if inferior)
  • Reperfusion rhythm: classically AIVR, usually benign
Exam tip Failed reperfusion (ongoing pain, poor ST resolution, instability): immediate rescue PCI.
Safety Post-lytic headache, confusion or new deficit: suspect a brain bleed; stop antithrombotics, emergency head CT.

Complications

ComplicationTimingClues
VF or pulseless VTMost within 48 hDefibrillate per ACLS
Papillary muscle rupture3-5 daysInferior/lateral MI; pulmonary edema; murmur may be absent
Septal rupture (VSD)3-5 daysNew pansystolic murmur; O2 step-up RA to PA
Free wall rupture3-5 days; 24-48 h after lyticsTamponade, PEA; late reperfusion raises risk
Cardiogenic shockAny time5-10% of acute MI; 40-50% short-term death
  • Shock: emergency culprit PCI or CABG, any time; no routine non-culprit PCI
  • Impella: reasonable in selected STEMI shock; more bleeding, limb ischemia and renal replacement (such as CRRT)
  • Routine IABP or VA-ECMO: no survival benefit
Safety New murmur, flash pulmonary edema or collapse days after MI: urgent echo, surgical center, MCS as a bridge.
