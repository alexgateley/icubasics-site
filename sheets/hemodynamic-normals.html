Hemodynamic Normals

Normal adult values, key formulas and how to read the waveforms behind them

Checked against current sources October 2026

1 WU = 80 dyn·s/cm⁵; PH guidelines call PVR above 2 WU (160) high.

  • MAP = (SBP + 2 × DBP) ÷ 3
  • CO = HR × SV ÷ 1000 (L/min); CI = CO ÷ BSA
  • SVR = (MAP − CVP) × 80 ÷ CO
  • PVR = (mean PA − PAOP) × 80 ÷ CO
  • CaO2 = 1.34 × Hb × SaO2 (decimal) + 0.003 × PaO2 (17–20 mL/dL)
  • DO2 = CO × CaO2 × 10
  • Coronary perfusion = DBP − PAOP (60–80 mmHg)
Exam tip Low SvO2 or ScvO2: delivery is not meeting demand; check CO, hemoglobin, SaO2 and oxygen consumption. ScvO2 runs slightly below SvO2 in health, about 7% above it in unstable patients.

Arterial line and square-wave test

Square-wave test: snap the fast flush and count oscillations before the tracing returns.

Exam tip MAP stays the most reliable number on a damped line; confirm with a cuff while you fix it.

CVP and PA catheter waveforms

  • PAOP estimates LA pressure and LVEDP; it reads high with mitral disease or high PEEP, low with a stiff LV.
  • PAOP sits a few mmHg below PAD; a wide gap suggests high PVR. Small gap, stable patient: trend PAD and wedge less.
  • Cannon a waves: AV dissociation. No a waves: atrial fibrillation.
  • Giant v waves: tricuspid (CVP) or mitral (wedge) regurgitation.
Safety Check the PA trace before inflating; if damped, don't. Inflate slowly and stop at wedge, never past the 1.5 mL marked; wedge at under 1.25 mL suggests distal migration. Wedge 2 breaths or less (10–15 s), deflate passively, never flush while wedged. Wedge or RV trace with balloon down: catheter has moved (wedge risks infarction). Act per protocol.

Reading the numbers

  • Level the air-fluid interface to the phlebostatic axis (4th intercostal space, midway front to back); re-level when position changes.
  • Zero to air. Head of bed 0–60° is fine once leveled.
  • Read at end-expiration on a frozen or printed trace, not the digital number: just before the inspiratory dip (spontaneous) or rise (ventilator).
  • Read CVP and PAOP at the mean of the a wave.
Safety A transducer below the phlebostatic axis reads falsely high; above it reads falsely low, about 2 mmHg per inch.

Fluid responsiveness

  • Responder: SV or CO rises more than 10% with a fluid challenge or passive leg raise (PLR).
  • PLR: from 45° semi-recumbent, tilt the bed to trunk flat, legs up. Judge by SV or CO within 1–2 minutes, not BP.
  • PPV above 13% or SVV above about 12% predicts response.
  • PPV and SVV need controlled ventilation, no spontaneous breaths, regular rhythm, tidal volume about 8 mL/kg IBW, closed chest, normal abdominal pressure.
  • SSC 2026: dynamic measures over physical exam or static values alone. Septic shock: initial MAP 65 mmHg (60–65 if 65 or older).
Exam tip CVP and PAOP are static and poorly predict fluid response; exams favor PLR, SVV and PPV. For shock profiles, see Shock Types. Study values only; follow your institution's protocols.
