Heart Failure

HFrEF vs HFpEF, stages, GDMT pillars, warm/cold profiles, shock and diuretics

Checked against current sources October 2026

Types, stages and NYHA class

2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA cut-offs. Finerenone, a nonsteroidal MRA, is FDA-approved (2025) for LVEF ≥40%, not HFrEF.
TypeLVEFExam point
HFrEF≤40%Four pillars cut deaths
HFmrEF41–49%SGLT2i; MRA or ARNi may help
HFpEF≥50%SGLT2i; treat BP, AF, congestion
HFimpEFWas ≤40%, now >40%Keep GDMT
StageMeaning
AAt risk (HTN, diabetes, obesity, CAD, cardiotoxins); no structural disease
BPre-HF: structural change or high BNP; never symptomatic
CStructural disease with current or past symptoms
DAdvanced: marked symptoms, repeat admissions despite optimized GDMT
  • NYHA I: no limits from HF
  • NYHA II: symptoms with ordinary activity
  • NYHA III: symptoms with less than ordinary activity
  • NYHA IV: symptoms at rest or with any activity
Exam tip Stages never move backward: stage C stays C even when symptoms resolve. NYHA class can improve or worsen.

Left vs right failure

Left-sided (lungs)Right-sided (body)
Dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, fatigueJVD, hepatojugular reflux
Crackles, coughDependent pitting edema
S3 gallopHepatomegaly, ascites, early satiety
High PCWPHigh CVP

Left-sided failure is the most common cause of right-sided failure. Obesity lowers BNP and NT-proBNP.

HFrEF: the four pillars

Start all four unless contraindicated; titrate per your institution's protocols. Diuretics ease congestion but are not a pillar.

PillarExamplesWatch for
ARNi (preferred), ACEi or ARBSacubitril-valsartan, lisinopril, losartanLow BP, high K; 36 h ACEi washout before ARNi; no ARNi or ACEi after angioedema
Beta-blockerCarvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprololBradycardia; not started on IV inotropes; never stop abruptly
MRASpironolactone, eplerenoneStart if eGFR >30 and K <5.0; stop if K stays ≥5.5
SGLT2iDapagliflozin, empagliflozinNo diabetes needed; genital infection, volume loss, euglycemic DKA; hold ≥3 days before surgery

ACEi dry cough: switch to an ARNi or ARB. Add hydralazine-isosorbide dinitrate for self-identified African American patients with NYHA III–IV symptoms despite optimal therapy.

Exam tip Only carvedilol, bisoprolol and metoprolol succinate are proven in HFrEF; metoprolol tartrate is not. Sacubitril-valsartan raises BNP but not NT-proBNP, so follow NT-proBNP.

Decompensated HF and shock

Wet = congested (crackles, JVD, edema, weight gain). Cold = poorly perfused (cool skin, narrow pulse pressure, confusion, low urine output, rising lactate).

ProfilePictureTreatment focus
Warm-dryCompensatedTitrate oral GDMT
Warm-wetMost commonIV loop diuretic; vasodilator (nitroglycerin, nitroprusside) if BP allows
Cold-wetWorst outlookInotrope plus diuretic; MCS if failing
Cold-dryUncommonCautious fluids; inotrope if needed

Pulmonary edema: sit upright, give oxygen and an IV loop diuretic; track urine output, K, BP. Keep GDMT unless contraindicated (e.g., shock); hold or reduce the beta-blocker for marked overload or low output. Refractory congestion: ultrafiltration or CRRT.

Exam tip Milrinone (a PDE3 inhibitor) works despite beta-blockers. It vasodilates (watch BP) and needs a lower rate in renal impairment.
Safety Cardiogenic shock: SBP <90 mm Hg for >30 min or pressors needed, plus hypoperfusion (urine <30 mL/h, lactate >2 mmol/L); typically CI <2.2 and PCWP >15. Revascularize if MI. Norepinephrine for hypotension, plus an inotrope (dobutamine or milrinone) for low output. Temporary MCS (e.g., Impella, VA-ECMO) if drugs fail; routine IABP or ECMO is not advised in MI shock.

Diuretic monitoring

  • Daily standing weight, same time; strict intake and output
  • Daily K, Mg, Na, BUN and creatinine while titrating
  • BP and orthostatic symptoms; avoid over-diuresis
  • Push usual IV furosemide doses (20–80 mg) over 1–2 min; infuse high doses no faster than 4 mg/min
  • Tinnitus or hearing loss: ototoxicity (rapid or high doses, renal failure, aminoglycosides)
  • Poor response: higher IV loop dose, or add metolazone (watch K, Na)
  • Small creatinine rise with good decongestion: not a reason to stop

IV loop doses often start at ≥2× the home oral dose, per your institution's protocols.

Safety Low K or Mg raises the risk of digoxin toxicity. MRAs and ACEi/ARB/ARNi raise K; check labs before giving.

Patient teaching

  • Weigh each morning after voiding, before eating, same scale
  • Report a gain of 2–3 lb in a day or 5 lb in a week, or new dyspnea or swelling
  • Limit sodium (processed, canned, restaurant food); often 1,500–2,300 mg/day
  • Restrict fluids only if prescribed
  • Take GDMT daily even when well; never stop a beta-blocker suddenly
  • Avoid NSAIDs and potassium salt substitutes
  • Take the diuretic early in the day; rise slowly; stay active
