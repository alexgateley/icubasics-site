Modes: what you set vs what varies

Typical adult values for exam study, not a bedside tool; follow your institution's protocols. PEEP and FiO₂ are set in every mode.

ModeYou setVariesKnow
AC/VCVt, rate, flowPressureFull Vt every breath
AC/PCPressure, insp time, rateVtVt falls as lungs stiffen
PRVCTarget Vt, ratePressureAdjusts pressure to hit set Vt
SIMVRate, Vt or pressureVt of extra breathsWeans slower than SBTs
PSVPS levelVt, rate, insp timePatient-triggered; no set rate
CPAPOne pressureVt, rateNo inspiratory boost; SBTs
APRVP-high, P-low, T-high, T-lowVtRefractory hypoxemia; traps air in COPD
BiPAP (NIV)IPAP, EPAP by maskVtCOPD flare, cardiogenic edema
  • BiPAP: IPAP − EPAP = pressure support; widen the gap to blow off CO₂
  • For oxygenation, raise EPAP (acts like PEEP) and IPAP together to keep support
  • COPD flare with pH ≤7.35 and PaCO₂ >45: bilevel NIV (ERS/ATS)
Exam tip Exams swap these: stiff lungs raise pressure in volume modes (high-pressure alarm) but drop Vt in pressure modes (low-volume alarm).
Safety NIV needs an awake patient who protects the airway. Obtunded, heavy secretions, unstable BP or ileus: plan to intubate instead.

Lung-protective settings and ABG fixes

To fixChange
PaCO₂ and pHRate or Vt (minute ventilation = RR × Vt)
PaO₂ or SpO₂FiO₂ or PEEP
  • Vt 4–8 mL/kg predicted body weight (PBW); ARDS target 6
  • PBW: men 50 kg, women 45.5 kg, + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 feet
  • Plateau ≤30 cm H₂O on a 0.5-second inspiratory hold
  • Driving pressure = plateau − PEEP; aim under about 15 (observational data)
  • Paired PEEP/FiO₂ steps to SpO₂ 88–95% or PaO₂ 55–80
  • ARDS by P/F: mild 201–300, moderate 101–200, severe ≤100
  • Moderate–severe: higher PEEP, no prolonged recruitment maneuvers; prone over 12 h/day if P/F stays <150 (PROSEVA used ≥16 h)

Example: a 70-inch man's PBW is 73 kg whatever he weighs, so 6 mL/kg ≈ 440 mL. SSC 2026 (sepsis): 6 mL/kg with ARDS, 6–8 without.

Permissive hypercapnia (ARDSNet): pH goal 7.30–7.45; raise the rate first (max 35).

Peak vs plateau

Read these on volume breaths. In pressure modes peak is set, so watch exhaled Vt instead.

PatternMeansThink
High peak, normal plateau↑ Airway resistanceSecretions, biting, kink, bronchospasm
High peak and high plateau↓ ComplianceARDS, edema, pneumothorax, mainstem tube, tense abdomen
Low peakLeak or disconnectCircuit off, cuff leak, tube out
Exam tip Peak − plateau = resistive pressure (over about 10 cm H₂O is high). Suction, bite block or bronchodilator fixes resistance; poor compliance needs a cause hunt.

Auto-PEEP (air trapping)

  • Next breath starts before exhalation ends; air stacks up
  • Clue: expiratory flow does not return to zero
  • Measure: end-expiratory hold; total PEEP − set PEEP = auto-PEEP
  • Risks: hypotension, barotrauma, missed triggers
  • At risk: asthma, COPD, high rate or minute ventilation
  • Fix: longer exhale (lower rate, shorter inspiration, smaller Vt); treat bronchospasm
Safety Air trapping with severe hypotension or PEA: briefly disconnect the ventilator to let air out, then resume at a lower rate.

Alarms and sudden distress: DOPE

  • Displacement: tube depth, capnography, breath sounds
  • Obstruction: kink, biting, mucus plug; pass a suction catheter
  • Pneumothorax: one-sided breath sounds, rising pressures, falling BP
  • Equipment: disconnection, leak, O₂ supply, ventilator failure
Safety Distress with no obvious cause: disconnect and bag with 100% O₂ while you work through DOPE. Check the patient before silencing alarms.

Ready to wean? Spontaneous breathing trial

Screen daily, paired with a sedation awakening trial.

  • Cause improving; triggers own breaths; no paralytic
  • FiO₂ ≤0.40 and PEEP ≤8; SBP ≥90 off vasopressors (ARDSNet)
  • Trial 30–120 min on PS 5–8 cm H₂O, CPAP or T-piece; don't raise FiO₂
  • Stop for RR >35, SpO₂ <90%, pH <7.30, Vt <4 mL/kg, or 2+ distress signs: HR >120% of baseline, accessory muscles, paradox, sweating, marked dyspnea
  • High failure risk (older, COPD, heart failure, CO₂ rise on SBT): extubate to preventive NIV
  • Stridor risk: cuff leak test; no leak → steroids ≥4 h before extubation
Exam tip RSBI = RR ÷ Vt in liters; under 105 favors success. Still tested, though AARC 2024 says it isn't needed before an SBT.
