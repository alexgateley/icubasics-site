Airway and RSI

Assessment, adjuncts, RSI drugs, failed airway, tube checks, DOPE, pediatric tubes

Checked against current sources October 2026

Assess the airway

MnemonicPredictsLetters
LEMONHard laryngoscopyLook, 3-3-2, Mallampati, Obstruction/obesity, Neck
MOANSHard bag-maskMask seal, Obesity/obstruction, Age >55, No teeth, Stiff lungs/snoring

3-3-2 (fingers): mouth opening 3, chin to hyoid 3, hyoid to thyroid notch 2. Correct hypoxemia, hypotension and acidosis before induction.

Exam tip Exams test the match: LEMON for intubation, MOANS for bag-mask.

Adjuncts, bag-mask, SGAs

DeviceSizingAvoid if
OPAIncisors to jaw angle, or mouth corner to earGag or cough reflex
NPANose tip to earlobeBasal skull fracture, midface trauma, severe coagulopathy
i-gel3 = 30–60 kg, 4 = 50–90 kg, 5 = 90+ kgIntact gag, limited mouth opening
  • NPA suits semiconscious patients; OPA only with no gag reflex.
  • Bag-mask: two people, two-hand seal with jaw thrust (AHA 2025).
  • Just enough for chest rise, over 1 s; 1 breath every 6 s with a pulse.
  • No routine cricoid pressure in arrest or in children (AHA 2025).
Safety An SGA is not definitive: it does not fully protect against aspiration.

RSI: the 7 Ps

  1. Preparation: roles, backup devices, suction, IV, drugs.
  2. Preoxygenation: 3 min tidal or 8 vital-capacity breaths; NIV if hypoxemic; nasal cannula for apneic oxygenation.
  3. Pretreatment: fix hypotension and hypoxemia first.
  4. Paralysis with induction: induction agent, then paralytic at once.
  5. Positioning: head up, sniffing or ramped.
  6. Placement with proof: waveform capnography.
  7. Post-intubation care: secure, x-ray, sedation, ventilator.
Safety Failed airway: limit attempts (2–3) and oxygenate with an SGA or bag-mask. Can't intubate, can't oxygenate: cricothyrotomy per protocol.

RSI drugs

Typical adult IV doses for exam study; follow your agency's or institution's protocols.

Etomidate: no mortality difference, no routine steroids (SCCM 2023). Ketamine: similar mortality, more cardiovascular collapse (RSI trial 2025). In shock, lower doses; have vasopressors ready.

FeatureSuccinylcholineRocuronium
RSI dose1–1.5 mg/kg1–1.2 mg/kg
OnsetAbout 1 minAbout 1 min (under 2)
DurationUnder 10 min (peak 2, recovery 4–6)About 60–70 min; can exceed 2 h
  • Avoid succinylcholine with hyperkalemia, malignant hyperthermia risk, myopathy, or burns, crush, stroke or cord injury past the acute phase (risk peaks 7–10 days).
  • Children: boxed warning for hyperkalemic arrest from undiagnosed myopathy; reserve for emergency intubation. Bradycardia, worse with repeat doses.
  • Low pseudocholinesterase prolongs paralysis.

Sugammadex reverses rocuronium or vecuronium; 16 mg/kg reverses rocuronium immediately. Watch for anaphylaxis, bradycardia; backup contraception for 7 days.

Exam tip Exams pick rocuronium for burns or crush days old, stroke or cord injury, neuromuscular disease, or high potassium (missed dialysis).

Confirm the tube

  • Waveform capnography plus clinical assessment: most reliable (AHA 2025, Class 1 in arrest).
  • Sustained CO₂: a waveform every breath, peaks over 7.5 mm Hg, steady or rising (not fading), for at least 7 breaths.
  • Breath sounds can't rule out the esophagus; x-ray checks depth, not placement.
  • Depth at the incisors: about 20–21 cm women, 22–23 cm men.
Safety No trace = wrong place. Without sustained CO₂, the default is to remove the tube.

Deterioration and sedation

DOPECluesAct
DisplacementCO₂ lost, depth changed, one-sided soundsCheck depth, reposition
ObstructionHigh pressures; suction catheter won't passSuction, unkink, bite block
PneumothoraxOne-sided sounds, high pressures, low BPDecompress per protocol
EquipmentDisconnection, O₂ supply, vent faultBag by hand
Safety Sudden distress on the ventilator: disconnect and bag with oxygen while working through DOPE.
  • Analgesia first (usually an opioid), then sedation to a target.
  • Rocuronium outlasts induction agents: sedate promptly to prevent awareness with paralysis.

Pediatric tubes

ItemRule
Uncuffed, age 2+Age/4 + 4 mm
Cuffed, age 2+Age/4 + 3.5 mm
Depth (cm)3 × tube size; or age/2 + 12 (age 2+)
Cuff pressureUnder 20–25 cm H₂O; recheck at altitude
  • Cuffed over uncuffed is reasonable (AHA/AAP 2025). Up to 35 kg, a length-based tape beats age formulas; have half sizes up and down ready.
  • Atropine to prevent bradycardia may be considered before emergency intubation: 0.02 mg/kg, no minimum dose (AHA/AAP 2025).
Exam tip Four-year-old: cuffed 4.5 mm (4/4 + 3.5) at about 13.5 cm (3 × 4.5).
