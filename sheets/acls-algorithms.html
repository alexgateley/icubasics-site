ACLS Algorithms in Brief

2025 AHA adult arrest, bradycardia, tachycardia and post-ROSC steps on one page

Checked against current sources October 2026

Cardiac arrest: two tracks

Start CPR, oxygen and the monitor; ask: shockable? Recheck the rhythm every 2 min; pulse checks take 10 s at most.

StepVF / pulseless VTPEA / asystole
First moveShock, resume CPR at onceCPR and epinephrine ASAP
EpinephrineAfter initial shocks failAs soon as feasible
AntiarrhythmicAfter the 3rd shockNot used
Exam tip Exam order: shock, CPR, shock, epinephrine, shock, amiodarone or lidocaine. Epinephrine every 3–5 min is about every other 2-minute cycle.

CPR quality, shocks and drugs

  • Rate 100–120/min; depth 2–2.4 in (5–6 cm)
  • Full recoil; short pauses; compression fraction at least 60%; swap compressors every 2 min
  • 30:2 without an advanced airway; with one, 1 breath every 6 s and nonstop compressions
  • ETCO₂ under 10 mm Hg: improve CPR (aim for 20 or more). A sudden rise of more than 10 mm Hg may signal ROSC

Safety Not routine in arrest: calcium, bicarbonate, magnesium, vasopressin, high-dose epinephrine, pacing. Hyperkalemic arrest: calcium and bicarbonate may be considered (2025: benefit unproven).

Bradycardia with a pulse

Usually under 50/min. Treat if it causes hypotension, acute confusion, shock, ischemic chest pain or acute heart failure; look for ischemia, drugs, hypoxia, hyperkalemia.

  1. Airway, oxygen if needed, monitor, IV, 12-lead ECG
  2. Atropine 1 mg IV, repeated every 3–5 min to a 3 mg total
  3. If atropine fails: transcutaneous pacing and/or dopamine 5–20 mcg/kg/min or epinephrine 2–10 mcg/min
  4. Expert consult and transvenous pacing
Safety Shock and no IV or IO yet: pace while getting access. Heart transplant: no atropine (denervated heart; paradoxical block reported); go to pacing or an infusion.

Tachycardia with a pulse

Usually 150/min or more. Unstable because of the rhythm (not, say, sepsis)? Synchronized cardioversion, sedated when feasible; if regular and narrow, adenosine may be tried while preparing.

2025 biphasic starting energies; increase if a shock fails; re-sync each time.
Unstable rhythmStarting energy
Atrial fibrillation200 J or more
Atrial flutter200 J
Other narrow-complex100 J
Monomorphic VT100 J
Polymorphic VTDefibrillate (always unstable)
  • Stable, narrow, regular: vagal maneuvers, adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push with a flush, then 12 mg
  • Then a beta-blocker or calcium channel blocker (not diltiazem or verapamil in suspected systolic heart failure)
  • Stable, wide (QRS 0.12 s or more): adenosine only if regular and monomorphic
  • Amiodarone 150 mg over 10 min, then 1 mg/min for 6 h
  • Or procainamide 20–50 mg/min; stop at 17 mg/kg, hypotension or QRS widening over 50%; avoid in long QT or heart failure
Safety Can't synchronize a crashing patient? Shock unsynchronized. Torsades: IV magnesium, correct potassium. Adenosine: avoid in asthma; 1 mg may suffice with a central line or transplanted heart.

After ROSC

  • FiO₂ 100% until SpO₂ is reliable, then 90–98% (PaO₂ 60–105 mm Hg)
  • PaCO₂ 35–45 mm Hg (ETCO₂ may read falsely low)
  • MAP 65 mm Hg or more
  • Early 12-lead; emergent cath for STEMI, cardiogenic shock, recurrent VT/VF or severe ischemia
  • Not following commands: hold 32–37.5 °C for at least 36 h; rewarm no faster than 0.5 °C/h
  • Glucose 70–180 mg/dL; EEG for seizures
  • Prognosis: multimodal, at least 72 h after ROSC or normothermia
