When to transfuse

  • Stable hospitalized adults: RBCs below Hgb 7 g/dL (AABB 2023)
  • Cardiac surgery: below 7.5. Orthopedic surgery or existing heart disease: below 8
  • Acute MI: below 10 (AABB 2025, conditional)
  • Active bleeding or shock: treat the patient, not the number
  • Platelets (AABB/ICTMG 2025): below 10,000 chemo prophylaxis or compressible CVC; 20,000 LP; 50,000 major non-neuraxial surgery
  • Avoid platelets in HIT, TTP or ITP unless bleeding is life-threatening
Exam tip Exams default to 7, or 8 with heart disease. The AMI threshold of 10 is newer.

Giving blood safely

  1. Confirm order, consent and baseline vital signs
  2. Bedside check per policy: two identifiers, unit number, ABO/Rh, expiration
  3. Blood set with a 150 to 260 micron filter, saline primed
  4. Start slowly; stay close for 15 minutes, then recheck vitals
  5. Finish within 4 hours of spiking the bag
  6. Warmer for rapid or massive transfusion
Safety Only 0.9% sodium chloride or an FDA-approved blood-compatible fluid (such as Plasma-Lyte A). Never Lactated Ringer's (calcium clots citrated blood), dextrose or medications.

NCLEX-PN tests monitoring transfusions; LPN/LVN roles in starting one vary by state and facility.

Exam tip TACO or TRALI? TACO: high BP and BNP; diuretics help. TRALI: may bring fever and low BP; diuretics do not help.

Massive transfusion

  • Often 10 or more RBC or whole blood units in 24 hours
  • 1:1:1 plasma, platelets, RBCs: better hemostasis, fewer early bleeding deaths (PROPPR)
  • Citrate binds calcium (long QTc, tetany, paresthesias): check ionized calcium
  • Military: 1 g calcium (10 mL 10% chloride or 30 mL 10% gluconate) with the first unit, then every 4 units; gluconate is safer peripherally
  • Prevent hypothermia, acidosis and coagulopathy; watch potassium
  • TXA (CRASH-2): 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 h, within 3 h of injury. Military guidance: one 2 g bolus
Exam tip TXA timing is testable: benefit was greatest within 1 hour; starting after 3 hours raised bleeding deaths.

Typical adult doses for exam study; follow your institution's or agency's protocols.
